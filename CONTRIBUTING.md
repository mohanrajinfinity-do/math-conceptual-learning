# Contributing to Math Conceptual Learning

Thank you for your interest in contributing to Math Conceptual Learning.

This project aims to create practical, open educational resources that help students develop stronger conceptual understanding in mathematics.

Contributions from teachers, educators, mathematics students, and others interested in mathematics education are welcome.

## Ways You Can Contribute

You can contribute by:

- Suggesting common mathematical misconceptions
- Improving existing explanations
- Adding diagnostic questions
- Suggesting remediation activities
- Sharing classroom strategies
- Identifying errors or unclear explanations
- Suggesting new mathematics topics
- Improving the documentation

## Guidelines for Educational Resources

When contributing a resource, please try to include:

1. The mathematical concept
2. The common misconception
3. Possible reasons for the misconception
4. A diagnostic question or activity
5. A conceptual explanation
6. A suggested remediation strategy
7. A way to reassess student understanding

The goal is to understand student thinking rather than simply identify whether an answer is correct or incorrect.

## Quality and Accuracy

All mathematical content should be checked carefully before submission.

Please:

- Verify calculations and mathematical statements
- Use clear and age-appropriate language
- Avoid unnecessary complexity
- Explain the reasoning behind solutions
- Use examples where they improve understanding

## Classroom Experience

If you are sharing an idea based on classroom experience, please avoid including personally identifiable information about students.

Do not include:

- Student names
- Contact information
- Photographs without appropriate permission
- Personal or sensitive student information

## Suggestions and Feedback

If you have an idea for improving the project, you can open an issue and describe:

- What you would like to improve
- Why it would be useful
- An example, if possible

Constructive feedback is welcome.

## Contribution Philosophy

This project is intended to grow gradually through practical teaching experience and contributions from the education community.

The aim is to create resources that are simple, useful, evidence-informed, and easy for teachers to adapt to their classrooms.

Thank you for helping improve mathematics learning.
