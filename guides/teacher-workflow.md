# Teacher Workflow

## Purpose

This guide explains how teachers can use the resources in Math Conceptual Learning to identify, address, and reassess mathematical misconceptions.

The project follows a simple cycle:

**Identify → Diagnose → Explain → Remediate → Reassess → Reflect**

---

## Step 1: Identify

Start by selecting a mathematical concept.

Examples:

- Rational Numbers
- Integers
- Fractions

Look through the relevant misconception resource and identify difficulties that may be relevant to your students.

---

## Step 2: Diagnose

Use a diagnostic question to explore the student's understanding.

Do not focus only on whether the answer is correct.

Ask the student:

- "How did you get your answer?"
- "Why do you think that?"
- "Can you show me another way?"
- "Can you represent it using a diagram or number line?"

The student's reasoning can help reveal the underlying misconception.

---

## Step 3: Record the Observation

Use the Teacher Observation Template to record:

- The question
- Student response
- Student reasoning
- Observed misconception
- Possible reason for the difficulty

Avoid recording personally identifiable student information in publicly shared material.

---

## Step 4: Explain

Choose an explanation that connects the concept to the student's existing understanding.

Possible approaches include:

- Visual representations
- Number lines
- Concrete examples
- Real-life situations
- Mathematical models
- Guided questioning

The aim is to develop conceptual understanding rather than encourage memorisation of rules.

---

## Step 5: Remediate

Select or design an activity that directly addresses the identified misconception.

For example:

### Rational Numbers

Use visual models to compare fractions.

### Integers

Use number lines to represent positive and negative movement.

### Fractions

Use fraction strips or area models to demonstrate equivalent fractions.

---

## Step 6: Reassess

Give the student a similar but different question.

For example:

Initial question:

**Which is greater: 1/4 or 1/8?**

Follow-up:

**Which is greater: 1/3 or 1/6?**

Ask the student to explain the reasoning again.

The aim is to determine whether the student can apply the concept independently.

---

## Step 7: Reflect

Record:

- What changed?
- What evidence showed improvement?
- What difficulty remains?
- What should be tried next?

This information can be used to improve future teaching and resources.

---

---

## Important Principle

A wrong answer does not always tell us why a student is struggling.

The purpose of this project is to look beyond the answer and understand the student's thinking.

**The goal is not just to correct mistakes. The goal is to understand and improve mathematical thinking.**
