# Rational Numbers – Remediation Strategies

## Purpose

This guide provides simple classroom strategies for helping students overcome common misconceptions about rational numbers.

The focus is on building conceptual understanding rather than asking students to memorize rules.

---

## 1. Misconception: A Larger Denominator Means a Larger Fraction

### Example

A student thinks:

1/8 > 1/4

because 8 > 4.

### Remediation Strategy

Use visual models.

Draw two equal-sized rectangles.

- Divide the first into 4 equal parts.
- Divide the second into 8 equal parts.
- Shade one part in each rectangle.

Ask:

**Which shaded part is larger? Why?**

### Key Idea

When the same whole is divided into more equal parts, each part becomes smaller.

Therefore:

1/4 > 1/8

---

## 2. Misconception: Fractions Are Only Numbers Written as Two Whole Numbers

### Remediation Strategy

Connect fractions to real situations.

Examples:

- Half of a pizza
- One-quarter of a litre
- Three-quarters of a metre
- Half an hour

Ask students to describe what the numerator and denominator represent in each situation.

### Key Idea

A fraction represents a quantity or relationship, not simply two numbers written one above another.

---

## 3. Misconception: Equivalent Fractions Are Different Quantities

### Example

A student may think:

1/2 ≠ 2/4

because the numbers are different.

### Remediation Strategy

Use fraction strips or drawings.

Show:

1/2

and

2/4

using the same whole.

Ask:

**Do they represent the same amount?**

### Key Idea

Different fraction representations can describe the same quantity.

Therefore:

1/2 = 2/4

---

## 4. Misconception: Negative Fractions Can Be Compared Like Positive Fractions

### Example

A student may think:

-1/2 > -1/3

because 2 is greater than 3 is false, or may struggle to compare the signs and values.

### Remediation Strategy

Use a number line.

Place:

-1/2 and -1/3

on the number line and ask which number is farther to the right.

### Key Idea

On a number line, numbers farther to the right are greater.

Therefore:

-1/3 > -1/2

---

## 5. Teacher Questioning Strategy

Instead of immediately saying that an answer is wrong, ask:

- "How did you get your answer?"
- "Can you show it using a diagram?"
- "Can you explain it in another way?"
- "Where would this number be on a number line?"
- "Can you think of a real-life example?"
- "What would happen if we changed the denominator?"

These questions help reveal the student's thinking.

---

## 6. Reassessment

After remediation, give the student a similar but different problem.

Compare:

- Original response
- Reasoning before remediation
- Response after remediation
- Reasoning after remediation

The purpose is to check whether the student's conceptual understanding has changed.

---

## Teacher Reflection

After using a remediation strategy, record:

1. What misconception was observed?
2. What strategy was used?
3. How did the student respond?
4. What evidence showed improvement?
5. What should be tried next?

This information can be used to improve future versions of the resource.
